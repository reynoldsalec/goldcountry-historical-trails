# Sources

Narrative and rights for every source the pipeline touches. The machine-readable manifest
is `data/sources/sources.yml`; the per-sheet topographic index is
`data/sources/topo_index.csv` and its rendered form is `docs/topo-editions.md`.

Research covers historical foot trails throughout Nevada and Placer Counties from
1950 to the present. Inventory evidence by geographic coverage and decade, including
areas where no usable source has yet been located. Local examples below document
earlier research; they do not set countywide priorities. Pre-1950 collections are
background sources, not a required mapping phase.

`docs/topo-editions.md` is generated by the current acquisition script and still
highlights a legacy "Tier 1" subset. Use its countywide index for project coverage;
that subset is not the digitizing scope. See README §2 for the current scope and
`docs/open-questions.md` for remaining tooling changes.

Anything not already in the README §8 inventory needs an entry here before use
(README §8). Entries so far:

## US Census Bureau TIGERweb — county boundaries

- **Used for:** `data/sources/aoi_counties.geojson`, the Nevada and Placer County
  project boundary for research, raster acquisition, clipping, and digitizing.
- **Service:** `https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov/arcgis/rest/services/TIGERweb/State_County/MapServer`,
  layer 55 (`Counties` under the `Census 2020` group).
- **Vintage:** Census 2020, pinned deliberately. TIGERweb's layer 1 is a rolling "current"
  vintage; querying it would make the AOI silently drift. Both layers returned identical
  vertex counts for these two counties when checked on 2026-08-29.
- **Rights:** public domain (US federal government work).
- **Attribution:** "County boundaries: US Census Bureau TIGER/Line".
- **Rebuild:** `make fetch-aoi`. Idempotent — it leaves the file untouched when the
  fetched geometry matches what is committed, so the retrieval date does not churn.
- **Not evidence.** This is a reference boundary for scoping work. It is not a trail
  observation and never becomes one.

## OpenStreetMap (Overpass API) — legacy Bear River Canal work area

- **Used for:** `data/sources/aoi_tier1.geojson`, an optional legacy work-area reference.
  It does not restrict digitizing elsewhere in either county.
- **Endpoint:** `https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter`.
- **Snapshot:** pinned via an Overpass attic query, `[date:"2026-08-30T00:00:00Z"]`. OSM is
  a live database; without the pin the AOI would drift as people edit. Bump the date
  deliberately, never incidentally.
- **Ways used:** `Bear River Canal` (waterway=canal, 14 ways, merges to one 20.77 mi
  line), `Bear River Canal Trail` (highway=path, 8 ways, 5.03 mi, surface=dirt),
  `Bowman Feeder Canal` (waterway=canal, 3 ways, 1.04 mi). Endpoints are the canal's
  crossings with `Crother Road` and `Placer Hills Road`.
- **Rights:** ODbL. Attribution required: "© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL". This
  string must appear in the viewer's attribution control if any OSM-derived layer is
  shown (AGENTS.md §2.7).
- **Rebuild:** `make fetch-aoi`. Buffer is `--buffer` on `fetch_aoi.py tier1`, default
  250 m.
- **Not evidence.** OSM is not an authoritative record of historical trail extent. This
  boundary records a previous work area; it never establishes what was there. No OSM way may
  become an alignment in `data/authoritative/` (AGENTS.md §2.1).

### Corridor facts established from the snapshot

Chainage is measured along the merged `Bear River Canal` line.

| Feature | Canal chainage |
| --- | --- |
| Crother Road crossing | 14.75 mi |
| Meadow Gate Road crossing | 17.50 mi |
| Placer Hills Road crossing | 19.77 mi |
| Named trail extent | 14.65 – 19.78 mi |

The corridor cut is 14.75 → 19.77 mi, 5.03 mi long. This ordering — Crother, then Meadow
Gate, then Placer Hills — records how the legacy work area was constructed.
`Placer Hills Road` crosses the canal three times (13.00, 19.77,
19.86 mi), so the builder picks the crossing pair that best brackets the named trail
rather than the first one it finds.

Half the `Bear River Canal Trail` ways carry `access=private`, and gravel/unpaved
`highway=service` ways run alongside the canal — consistent with the berm/bank
distinction in AGENTS.md §6. Recorded as orientation. It is not evidence of anything.


## USGS Historical Topographic Map Collection (TNM Access API)

- **Used for:** `data/sources/topo_index.csv` (the manifest) and `data/raw/topo/` (the
  rasters). This is the backbone source for the whole project.
- **API:** `https://tnmaccess.nationalmap.gov/api/v1/products`, dataset
  `Historical Topographic Maps`, `prodFormats=GeoTIFF`. Human browse interface:
  [topoView](https://ngmdb.usgs.gov/topoview/).
- **Coverage:** 615 sheets intersect the acquisition AOI, 6.3 GB, dated 1888–2000. 91 of
  them cover the legacy Bear River Canal work area (1.0 GB). Scales present: 1:24,000 (414 sheets),
  1:125,000 (80), 1:62,500 (80), 1:31,680 (17), 1:250,000 (14), 1:100,000 (9),
  1:96,000 (1). Per-edition detail is in `docs/topo-editions.md`.
- **Legacy work-area example:** the 7.5-minute quads covering the corridor are **Auburn**,
  **Colfax** and **Lake Combie**. The 15-minute quads are **Auburn**, **Colfax** and
  **Grass Valley**. These are a subset of the countywide index, not a required first
  batch. Chicago Park does not reach this corridor but remains in scope where its
  sheet coverage intersects either county.
- **Rights:** public domain, a US federal government work. The FGDC `useconst` field
  reads "None". Publishable as a tile layer.
- **Attribution:** "Historical topographic maps: U.S. Geological Survey".
- **Already georeferenced.** These arrive as GeoTIFFs with a NAD27 polyconic
  projection. Reproject and tile only — do not re-georeference (AGENTS.md §3).
- **Rebuild:** `make fetch-topo`. Narrow the download to the corridor with
  `make fetch-topo TOPO=--tier1`. The index is always built county-wide so the two
  scopes cannot diverge; `download` filters it by the legacy `in_tier1` column. Use the
  default countywide scope for the project inventory. The download includes pre-1950
  sheets; acquisition is not evidence that their content applies after 1950.

### The printed date of a sheet is not the date of the ground it shows

This is the single most important thing to know about this source, and getting it wrong
would put a wrong date on evidence.

USGS reprinted and photo-revised a quadrangle for decades without changing the date
printed on it. **Nine** distinct scans of "Auburn, CA 1953" are published. They share a
title and an API `publicationDate`, and they show different ground:

| Scan | Date on map | Imprint | Photo revision | Aerial photo | Shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `CA_Auburn_288101_1953_24000` | 1953 | 1955 | — | 1952 | 1952 |
| `CA_Auburn_288100_1953_24000` | 1953 | 1961 | — | 1952 | 1952 |
| `CA_Auburn_302316_1953_24000` | 1953 | 1977 | 1973 | 1973 | 1973 |
| `CA_Auburn_100355_1953_24000` | 1953 | 1981 | 1981 | 1978 | 1978 |

A trail visible only on the last of these is evidence about 1978, not 1953. Recording it
as 1953 would be an unsupported claim of exactly the kind AGENTS.md §2.1 forbids — and
it is the kind of error an opposing attorney would find.

**149 of the 615 indexed sheets depict ground later than their printed date.**

`scripts/fetch_topoview.py index` therefore harvests each sheet's FGDC metadata record
(from the API's `vendorMetaUrl`, never a constructed URL) and stores the lineage dates
per sheet: `date_on_map`, `imprint_year`, `aerial_photo_year`, `field_check_year`,
`photo_revision_year`, `edit_year`. It also derives `content_year` — the latest of the
revision, aerial and field-check years — as the most recent ground condition a sheet can
attest to. Both are recorded; neither is chosen for you. **Date an observation from these
columns, never from the sheet title.**

The metadata also carries `woodland_tint`, which matters for digitizing: a tinted sheet
prints green over forested ground and can bury a thin trail line.

### Coverage across the study period

Compare editions for each quadrangle across both counties, retaining gaps by decade.
The indexed historical collection ends in 2000, so additional sources are needed to
cover the period through the present. Do not treat the collection's date range as
continuous coverage of every place or of foot trails.

For the legacy corridor example, Auburn, Colfax, and Lake Combie have original surveys
from 1949–1953 and photo revisions from 1973. The relevance of each edition to a trail
must be established from the sheet. The same source comparison method applies
throughout both counties; no special 1967–1972 window is required.

## USGS EarthExplorer and UCSB FrameFinder — aerial frames

Both are recorded in `data/sources/sources.yml` as **candidates only**. No frames have
been identified and none are cited, because neither index could be queried without a
step this repo cannot take on its own:

- **EarthExplorer** requires a free USGS ERS account, and the M2M API requires a token.
  No credentials are held here and none may be committed (AGENTS.md §4.4).
- **FrameFinder** is an Esri Web AppBuilder application whose `config.json` exposes no
  queryable service endpoint, so its flights cannot be enumerated automatically.

The earlier investigation left frame identification unresolved. Plan searches across
both counties for the 1950s onward, prioritizing gaps in map coverage. Record verified
flight dates and footprints before selecting frames. See `docs/open-questions.md`.

Aerial frames arrive as raw scans and need manual GCPs committed to `data/sources/gcp/`
(AGENTS.md §3). FrameFinder rights vary per flight; under AGENTS.md §2.7 a flight with
unknown rights may be digitized against but its imagery must not be published as a tile
layer.

## David Rumsey Map Collection — pre-1950 background

Three sheets were located and verified. All are CC-BY-NC-SA: attribute, and do not use
commercially (AGENTS.md §2.7). None has been georeferenced or examined for corridor
content yet — they are candidates, not evidence.

| Sheet | Date | Scale | Rumsey ID |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hartwell, *Map of Nevada County, California* | 1880 | 1:79,200 | `RUMSEY~8~1~200202~3000113` |
| Goddard, *Britton & Rey's Map of the State of California* | 1857 | 1:1,520,640 | `RUMSEY~8~1~188~10036` |
| C.F. Weber & Co., *Weber's Map of Placer County, California* | 1914 | 1:177,000 | `RUMSEY~8~1~254191~5519294` |

These records are retained from earlier research. They do not establish foot-trail
extent in 1950 or later. A mapped mining ditch or road is not itself evidence of a
foot trail. Britton & Rey 1857 is too coarse for segment-level tracing and remains a
background reference. Finding additional 19th-century sheets or GLO plats is not a
prerequisite for the current project.

Rumsey sheets are georeferenced via Allmaps from their IIIF manifests (README §4), which
are recorded per sheet in `data/sources/sources.yml`.
